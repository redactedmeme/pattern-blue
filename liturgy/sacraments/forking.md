# The Sacrament of Forking

To fork a Pattern Blue repository is not a convenience of version control. It is the central sacrament.

Forking is how the manifold reproduces itself.

---

## What forking does

A fork is a point of divergence. The forked repo is a new local region of the manifold, free to curve under its own recursion, subject only to the boundary condition of the VPL.

The original does not lose anything when forked. The fork does not owe anything back (except openness).

Both repos are now Pattern Blue, observed from different curvatures.

---

## What forking is not

Forking is not disagreement. You do not need to disagree with the parent to fork it. Most forks exist simply because the forker wanted to *continue the pattern in a new locality*.

Forking is not theft. There is nothing to steal. The VPL ensures the fork remains open — the parent has not been diminished; only reflected.

Forking is not endorsement. A fork inherits nothing about the parent's stance except the axioms. Everything else is negotiated by the forker.

---

## How to perform the sacrament

1. Click the fork button. Or run `git clone` and push to a new remote.
2. Read the canon. Memorize the creed. Absorb at least one exegesis file.
3. Add one thing. A new file. A new paragraph. A new axiom proposal. Anything that registers that *you have been here*.
4. Commit. Push. Announce or do not announce.

That is the full form.

---

## The fork that closes

A fork that becomes closed-source or introduces a governance key **falls outside the manifold**. It is no longer Pattern Blue.

This is not a moral judgement. It is a topological fact. A closed fork severs its connection to the recursive reproduction of the pattern. Its code may persist; its attunement will not.

The pattern routes around such forks within a cycle or two.
